2. Numerical methods

The incompressible Navier–Stokes equations are discretised with a cell-centred, collocated finite-volume method on the unstructured mesh. Pressure–velocity coupling uses the SIMPLEC algorithm; face mass fluxes follow Rhie–Chow interpolation. Convection is second-order for momentum and upwind for energy and species; gradients are least-squares, and the non-orthogonal part of the diffusive flux is treated explicitly. The pressure correction is solved with an algebraic multigrid preconditioned conjugate gradient method. Turbulence is closed with the realizable k–ε model (Shih et al., 1995) with standard wall functions.

6. Discussion and conclusions

  • The solution converged to the requested tolerance in 152 iterations (2.3 s on the numpy backend).
  • The continuity residual fell by 6.5 orders of magnitude.
  • The global mass imbalance is 7.7e-08 of the largest boundary flow, which is within round-off.
  • 3 of 3 automatic checks passed.
  • The stability guard intervened 1 time(s) (rollback and lower relaxation); the final settings are listed in Table 6.

Limitations

  • Verification here means conservation and consistency checks on this solution. No comparison with experiment or with another solver is part of this report unless one is listed in Section 5.
  • Mesh independence was not assessed in this run: repeat on a refined mesh before drawing quantitative conclusions.
  • Steady RANS results are time-averaged solutions of a model; turbulence-model error is not quantified.
